# Expired TLS Certificate Breaks Settlement File Transfer

    **Incident ID:** INC-2025-0228  **Severity:** SEV-2  **Date:** 2025-02-28  **Duration:** 3h 05m
    **Affected systems:** Settlement Batch Service, SFTP Bridge
    **Status:** Closed - post-incident review complete

    ## Summary
    The nightly settlement file to the clearing house failed because the client certificate used by the SFTP bridge expired at 00:00 UTC. Settlement was delayed by one cycle.

    ## Timeline (UTC)
    - 00:00 Certificate expires
- 00:15 Batch job fails with TLS handshake error; alert routed to a deprecated Slack channel
- 02:40 Finance operations notices missing settlement confirmation and pages Payments on-call
- 03:05 New certificate issued and deployed; batch re-run succeeds

    ## Root cause
    **Expired certificate with no expiry monitoring.** The certificate was manually issued in 2024 and never registered in the certificate inventory, so the 30-day expiry alarm never covered it.

    ## Contributing factors
    - Alerting for the batch job pointed to a Slack channel archived in November 2024.
- Runbook did not include the certificate renewal procedure.

    ## Customer impact
    Payment authorisations and transfers were delayed or declined for the duration noted above.
    Complaints were handled by the Contact Centre using runbook RB-PAY-004. No funds were lost.

    ## Remediation and action items
    - [DONE] Register all payments certificates in cert-inventory with 30/14/7-day alerts
- [DONE] Route batch alerts to PagerDuty
- [OPEN] Automate certificate rotation via Vault PKI (SEC-889)

    ## Lessons learned
    The incident review board re-emphasised that certificate expiry remains a systemic
    weakness in the payments estate and must be tracked at the quarterly reliability review.
